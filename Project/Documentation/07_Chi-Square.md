### Chi-square Test Results

- **Chi-square statistic**: 429.59
- **P-value**: 8.60e-93
- **Degrees of Freedom (dof)**: 3
- **Expected Frequencies**: The expected frequency table shows the expected counts for each combination of Stress Label and Depression Label, assuming there is no association between the two variables.

### Explanation

1. **Chi-square Statistic**:

   - The chi-square statistic of 429.59 is a measure of how much the observed frequencies (the actual counts in the data) deviate from the expected frequencies (what we would expect if there were no relationship between Stress Label and Depression Label).
   - A large value of the chi-square statistic indicates a significant deviation from the expected values, which suggests that there is a relationship between the two categorical variables.

2. **P-value**:

   - The p-value of 8.60e-93 is extremely small, much smaller than the typical significance threshold of 0.05.
   - A small p-value indicates that it is very unlikely that the observed differences between the actual and expected frequencies are due to random chance.

3. **Degrees of Freedom (dof)**:
   - The degrees of freedom for the chi-square test are 3, which is calculated based on the number of categories in both variables (Stress Label and Depression Label).

### Conclusion

Since the p-value is far smaller than 0.05, we **reject the null hypothesis**. This means that there is a **statistically significant association** between stress and depression in the data. In other words, the level of stress appears to be related to the level of depression, and this relationship is unlikely to have occurred by chance.

### Interpretation

- **Rejecting the null hypothesis** indicates that stress and depression are not independent of each other. There is a significant relationship between these two variables in the dataset.
- The chi-square statistic further supports this by showing a large deviation between the observed and expected counts for the various combinations of stress and depression levels.
